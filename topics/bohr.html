Bohr's Model of the Atom

Fixed energy shells K, L, M, N and why atoms do not collapse.

Stationary statesShellsEnergy levels
📘 Lesson🧪 Demo Lab🎯 Quick Quiz💬 Ask & Understand📘 Book Questions

📘Lesson — How Bohr Saved the Atom

Rutherford's model had a serious problem: a moving electron should lose energy and crash into the nucleus. In 1913 Niels Bohr proposed a new model to solve exactly this problem.

Bohr's main postulates

  1. Electrons revolve around the nucleus only in certain fixed circular paths. These are called stationary states, orbits, shells or energy levels.
  2. While an electron stays in an allowed orbit, its energy stays constant — it does not lose energy and does not fall into the nucleus. (That is why atoms are stable.)
  3. An electron can move from one orbit to another only by absorbing or releasing a fixed amount of energy — exactly equal to the difference in energy of the two shells. It cannot sit in the space between two shells.

The shells and their names

ShellKLMN
Shell number (n)1234
Positionclosest to nucleus→→farthest (of these four)
Energylowesthigherhigher stillhighest
  • The K-shell is closest to the nucleus, so it has the lowest energy.
  • Energy increases as we move away from the nucleus: K < L < M < N.
  • An electron jumps to a higher shell by absorbing energy, and falls back to a lower shell by releasing energy (as radiation).
💡
Why K, L, M, N and not A, B, C, D? These letters come from the X-ray naming system of the scientist Charles Barkla. He labelled the strongest X-ray line K, the next one L, and so on. Bohr borrowed those letters, so the first shell became K.
✅
Why Bohr's model worked: by saying that energy is constant inside an allowed shell, Bohr explained why electrons never spiral into the nucleus — and hence why atoms are stable. It could also explain the hydrogen spectrum, which Rutherford's model could not.

💬Ask & Understand

Q1What are the stationary orbits (shells) in Bohr's model?Very short answer▾

According to Bohr, electrons revolve around the nucleus in fixed circular paths called stationary states, orbits or shells, in which an electron does not lose energy. They are named K, L, M, N (n = 1, 2, 3, 4) with K closest to the nucleus.

Q2Name the shells in order from the nucleus, with their n values.Very short answer▾

K (n = 1) → L (n = 2) → M (n = 3) → N (n = 4). K is closest to the nucleus and has the least energy; energy increases as the shell moves farther out.

Q3R3 Q2. Which statements are correct or incorrect according to Bohr's model, and why?
(i) Electrons lose energy while moving in fixed orbits and slowly fall into the nucleus. (ii) Electrons can exist anywhere around the nucleus with no fixed energy. (iii) Electrons revolve in orbits of fixed energy without losing energy. (iv) Electrons can be found between energy levels as they move around the nucleus.Revise, Reflect, Refine · Q2▾

(i) INCORRECT. In Bohr's model an electron in a fixed orbit does not lose energy — the orbit is a stationary state, so the electron neither spirals inward nor falls into the nucleus.

(ii) INCORRECT. Electrons cannot float anywhere freely. They must be in fixed shells (K, L, M, N…), each with a definite fixed energy.

(iii) CORRECT. Electrons revolve in fixed circular orbits and do not lose energy there. This is exactly why Bohr's model could explain atomic stability.

(iv) INCORRECT. Electrons cannot exist between energy levels. They can only be in the fixed shells, and they move from one shell to another only by absorbing or releasing a fixed amount of energy — jumping directly, with no stop in between.

Q4R3 Q10. Both Rutherford's and Bohr's models have electrons orbiting the nucleus. Why did Rutherford's model fail to explain atomic stability while Bohr's succeeded?Revise, Reflect, Refine · Q10▾

Rutherford's model: electrons orbit freely, like planets around the Sun. But a particle moving in a circular path is constantly changing direction, so it is accelerating. An accelerating charged particle should continuously emit radiation. So the electron should lose energy, slow down, spiral inward and fall into the nucleus — atoms would collapse in a very short time. The model had no explanation for why this does not happen.

Bohr's model: Bohr introduced stationary states — fixed allowed orbits in which the electron's energy remains constant and no energy is radiated. This postulate removed the problem, so Bohr could explain why atoms are stable. His model could also explain observations such as the hydrogen spectrum.

📘Book Questions — Explained

1According to Bohr's model, how does an electron move from one shell to another, and how does the energy of shells change as we move away from the nucleus?

Electrons revolve in fixed circular paths called stationary states or shells (K, L, M, N for n = 1, 2, 3, 4). In a stationary state the electron does not lose energy.

Movement between shells: an electron moves from one shell to another by absorbing or releasing a fixed amount of energy equal to the difference in energy of the two shells. Absorb energy → jump to a higher (farther) shell; release energy → fall back to a lower shell.

Energy and distance: energy increases as we move away from the nucleus. The K-shell (n = 1) is closest and has the least energy; the L-shell has more than K, and so on.

Short answer
2Case-based: (a) Using 2n², calculate the maximum electrons in the L-shell. (b) How does Bohr's model explain the stability of atoms? State two key postulates. (c) What happens when an electron moves from a higher energy shell to a lower energy shell? / Name the shells in order with their n values.

(a) For the L-shell n = 2, so maximum electrons = 2 × (2)² = 2 × 4 = 8 electrons.

(b) (1) Electrons revolve only in fixed circular orbits (stationary states) and do not radiate energy while in them, so they never spiral into the nucleus. (2) The energy of an electron in a stationary state remains constant; energy is emitted or absorbed only when the electron changes shells.

(c) It releases a fixed amount of energy equal to the energy difference between the two shells. / Shells in order: K (n = 1) → L (n = 2) → M (n = 3) → N (n = 4).

Case-based question
3R3 Q6. Electrons move around the nucleus in orbits. Why do they not fly away from the atom? Explain what keeps them attracted to the nucleus.

Electrons do not fly away because they are held by the electrostatic force of attraction between the negative electrons and the positive protons in the nucleus. This force acts like the force that keeps a planet in orbit around the Sun — it continuously pulls the electron towards the centre and stops it from flying off in a straight line.

In Bohr's model this electrostatic attraction provides the centripetal force needed to keep the electron moving in its fixed circular orbit. As long as the electron stays in an allowed orbit, it does not lose energy, and it neither flies away nor falls in.
