The Gold Foil Experiment & Rutherford's Model

Alpha particles, surprising bounces, the nucleus and the discovery of the proton.

📘Lesson — Rutherford's Surprising Experiment

The experiment (1911)

Ernest Rutherford asked two young scientists, Geiger and Marsden, to fire alpha (α) particles at an extremely thin sheet of gold foil. An alpha particle is a tiny positively charged particle — actually the nucleus of a helium atom (2 protons + 2 neutrons, charge +2). A fluorescent screen (ZnS) was used to see where the particles went.

Before the experiment, everyone expected that — according to Thomson's model — the positive charge is spread evenly through the atom, so the fast alpha particles would pass straight through or deflect only a little.

💥
What actually happened:
  • Most alpha particles passed straight through without any deflection.
  • Some were sharply deflected at large angles.
  • A very few actually bounced straight back!
Rutherford said it was almost as unbelievable as firing a shell at a piece of tissue paper and having it bounce back and hit you.

What the observations mean

ObservationWhat it tells us about the atom
Most α-particles passed straight throughMost of the atom is empty space
A few were sharply deflectedThe positive charge is packed in a tiny region, not spread out
A very few bounced straight backThat region is extremely dense and carries all the positive charge and most of the mass

That tiny, dense, positively charged central region is called the nucleus.

Rutherford's nuclear model (the planetary model)

  • An atom is mostly empty space.
  • All the positive charge and almost all the mass sit in a very small central nucleus.
  • Electrons revolve around the nucleus in circular orbits, like planets going round the Sun.
HOW SMALL IS THE NUCLEUS? Diameter of atom ≈ 10−10 m  ·  Diameter of nucleus ≈ 10−15 m the nucleus is about 105 (one lakh) times smaller than the atom

If the atom were the size of a cricket ground, the nucleus would be about the size of a black pepper seed lying at the centre.

Limitation A: why atoms should collapse

An electron moving in a circular path is always changing direction, so it is accelerating. According to the physics of that time, an accelerating charged particle must continuously radiate energy. So the electron should keep losing energy, move in a smaller and smaller circle and finally fall into the nucleus. In reality atoms are stable and do not collapse. Rutherford's model could not explain this. 😔

Limitation B: no idea of electron arrangement

The model did not describe how the electrons are arranged around the nucleus, or what energies they have.

But it did give us the proton

Rutherford found that the nucleus carries positive charge, and identified the particles responsible for it: the protons. A proton has a charge equal in magnitude but opposite to an electron, taken as +1, and it is much heavier than an electron. For an atom to be neutral: number of protons = number of electrons.

AtomProtonsElectronsCharge balance
Helium (He)222 − 2 = 0 (neutral)
Sodium (Na)111111 − 11 = 0 (neutral)

💬Ask & Understand

Q1What is the gold foil experiment also known as, and who performed it?Very short answer▾

It is also called the α-ray (alpha ray) scattering experiment. It was performed by Geiger and Marsden in 1911 under the guidance of Ernest Rutherford.

Q2What is the nucleus of an atom?Very short answer▾

The nucleus is the extremely small, dense, positively charged region at the centre of an atom where almost the entire mass of the atom is concentrated. Its diameter is about 10−15 m, about 105 times smaller than the atom.

Q3How does one bounced-back alpha particle completely rule out Thomson's plum pudding model?Important reasoning▾

In the plum pudding model the positive charge is spread thinly and evenly through the whole atom, so the repulsion felt by an alpha particle at any point would be extremely weak. A fast, heavy alpha particle would sail straight through, at most bending slightly — it could never be thrown straight back.

The fact that a few alpha particles did bounce back sharply means they met something very small, extremely dense and strongly positively charged — the nucleus. So the positive charge cannot be spread out uniformly, and the plum pudding model is ruled out.

Q4What would happen if negatively charged particles were used instead of alpha particles in the gold foil experiment?Pause & Ponder▾

The gold nucleus is positive, so a negative particle would be attracted towards the nucleus instead of being repelled. Instead of bouncing back or deflecting at large angles, most particles would curve inwards towards the nucleus. The scattering pattern would be completely different.

Q5Assertion (A): Rutherford concluded that most of the mass of an atom is concentrated in a small region at the centre called the nucleus. Reason (R): According to Thomson's model, electrons are embedded in a uniformly distributed positive charge sphere. Choose the correct option.Assertion–Reason▾

Option (ii): Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.

A is true because the gold foil experiment proved that the mass and positive charge are packed in a tiny central nucleus. R is also true because Thomson's plum pudding model did say that electrons are embedded in a uniform positive sphere. But R only describes Thomson's earlier model — which Rutherford disproved — so it does not explain A.

📘Book Questions — Explained

1R3 Q1. Choose the correct option and explain the reason for the correct and incorrect options in the context of Rutherford's gold foil experiment:
(i) The experiment clearly showed the existence of neutrons in the nucleus. (ii) The results disproved the plum pudding model and led to the idea of a nucleus at the centre of the atom. (iii) The large deflection of a few alpha particles indicated that most of the mass of the atom and positive charge are packed into a tiny centre. (iv) The way alpha particles were deflected showed that electrons move around the nucleus.

(i) INCORRECT. Neutrons were discovered by Chadwick in 1932 through a different experiment. The gold foil experiment only revealed the nucleus.

(ii) CORRECT. The results could not be explained by the plum pudding model (spread-out positive charge), so Rutherford proposed a small, dense, positively charged nucleus at the centre.

(iii) CORRECT. A few alpha particles bouncing back is possible only if they hit a very small, extremely dense and strongly positive region — so mass and positive charge are packed into that tiny centre.

(iv) INCORRECT. The experiment told us about the nucleus — its size, charge and density — not about the movement of electrons. Electron orbits were a logical proposal of Rutherford's model, not a direct observation of the experiment.

Revise, Reflect, Refine · Q1
2R3 Q4. What conclusion did Rutherford draw about the position and characteristics of the atom's positively charged part from the few alpha particles that bounced back or were deflected at large angles?

The positive charge is not spread through the atom but is concentrated in an extremely small region at the centre — the nucleus.

  • The nucleus is extremely dense: it holds most of the atom's mass in a tiny volume.
  • It is very small: atom ≈ 10−10 m, nucleus ≈ 10−15 m, so the nucleus is about 105 times smaller.
  • Since most alpha particles passed straight through, most of the atom is empty space.
  • Only an alpha particle that came very close to the nucleus was repelled strongly enough to bounce back.
Revise, Reflect, Refine · Q4
3Case-based: Meghna studies the 1911 gold foil experiment. (a) What is an alpha particle and what is its charge? (b) State the three key observations and what each indicates. (c) Why is Rutherford's model called the planetary model? / State one limitation of the model.

(a) An alpha particle is the nucleus of a helium atom — 2 protons and 2 neutrons — and it carries a positive charge of +2.

(b) Most alpha particles passed straight through → the atom is mostly empty space. A few were sharply deflected → a small positive charge is concentrated in a tiny region. Very few bounced back → the nucleus is extremely dense and carries all the positive charge and most of the mass.

(c) It is called the planetary model because electrons revolve around the nucleus in circular paths, just as planets revolve around the Sun. Limitation: it could not explain the stability of the atom — a revolving electron should lose energy, spiral inward and fall into the nucleus, yet atoms are stable.
