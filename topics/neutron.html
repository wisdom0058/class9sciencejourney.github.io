📘Lesson — What Makes Up the Mass of an Atom?

The mass puzzle

By the 1920s scientists knew about protons and electrons. But a simple comparison did not add up:

  • A hydrogen atom has 1 proton.
  • A helium atom has 2 protons — so on that basis it should weigh about twice as much as hydrogen.
  • But helium weighs about 4 times as much as hydrogen!

Some extra mass was hiding inside the nucleus, and it carried no charge at all.

1932: Chadwick finds the neutron

James Chadwick, a student of Rutherford, discovered this new subatomic particle in 1932. It was named the neutron and is written as n⁰.

ParticleSymbolRelative chargeRelative massWhere it is found
Electrone⁻−11/1840 u (almost nothing)Outside the nucleus, in shells
Protonp⁺+11 uInside the nucleus
Neutronn⁰01 u (nearly equal to a proton)Inside the nucleus
🔍
Where are neutrons found? In the nucleus of all atoms except ordinary hydrogen, whose nucleus has only one proton and no neutron.
💪
Why the nucleus does not fly apart: protons are positive and should repel each other strongly. Neutrons help by (a) increasing the distance between protons, which weakens the repulsion, and (b) strengthening the nuclear force that pulls the nucleons together.

Nucleons

Protons and neutrons live together in the nucleus and are together called nucleons. Since the electron is about 1840 times lighter, the mass of an atom is basically the mass of its nucleons.

💬Ask & Understand

Q1Who discovered the neutron, in which year, and what is its charge?Very short answer▾

James Chadwick discovered the neutron in 1932. It is a neutral subatomic particle (charge 0) whose mass is nearly equal to that of a proton.

Q2How was the neutron discovered, and what role does it play in the nucleus?Short answer▾

Discovery: scientists noticed a puzzle — helium has 2 protons but its mass is about 4 times that of hydrogen (not twice). This suggested another particle that adds mass without adding charge. Chadwick found it in 1932 and named it the neutron (n⁰).

Role: neutrons are found in the nucleus of all atoms except hydrogen. Together with protons they make up the mass of the atom. They also reduce the repulsion between the positive protons by increasing the spacing between them and by strengthening the nuclear force that holds the nucleus together.

Q3What is the difference between atomic mass and mass number? Which particles decide the mass of an atom?Short answer▾

The mass number (A) is simply the count of protons + neutrons in one atom — it is a whole number. The atomic mass is the actual (average) mass of an atom measured in atomic mass units, which can be fractional, as in chlorine's 35.5 u.

The mass of an atom is decided by its protons and neutrons (the nucleons). Electrons are about 1840 times lighter, so their mass is ignored.

Q4Why is an atom electrically neutral even though it contains charged particles?Short answer▾

Because the number of protons equals the number of electrons. The total positive charge (+Z) exactly cancels the total negative charge (−Z), so the atom has no net charge. For example helium has 2 protons and 2 electrons, and sodium has 11 protons and 11 electrons.

📘Book Questions — Explained

1How was the neutron discovered? What role does it play in the nucleus?

The neutron was discovered in 1932 by James Chadwick, a student of Ernest Rutherford. Scientists had noticed that a helium atom has two protons yet its mass is about four times that of a hydrogen atom, not double. This suggested another particle in the nucleus that adds mass without adding charge. Chadwick discovered a new subatomic particle with a mass nearly equal to a proton but no electrical charge — the neutron, n⁰.

Neutrons are found in the nucleus of all atoms except hydrogen. Along with protons they contribute to the mass of the atom, and they help reduce the repulsion between the positively charged protons by increasing the spacing between them and strengthening the nuclear force holding the nucleus together.

Long answer
2Draw / describe the fundamental atomic structure showing all three subatomic particles and state the charge, mass and location of each.

An atom has a tiny central nucleus containing protons (charge +1, mass 1 u) and neutrons (charge 0, mass 1 u), and around it move electrons (charge −1, mass about 1/1840 u) in fixed shells. Almost the whole mass sits in the nucleus, while almost the whole volume is empty space.

Question bank
3R3 Q7. Assertion (A): The discovery of subatomic particles helped in understanding the atomic structure. Reason (R): The number of electrons is equal to the number of protons in an atom.

Option (ii): Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.

A is true — the discovery of electrons, protons and neutrons led step by step to better models of the atom. R is also true — in a neutral atom the number of electrons always equals the number of protons. But the equal number of electrons and protons only tells us about charge balance; it does not explain how the discovery of subatomic particles improved our understanding of atomic structure.
