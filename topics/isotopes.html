Isotopes, Isobars & Average Atomic Mass

📘Lesson — Same Element, Different Mass

Isotopes

Isotopes are atoms of the same element that have the same atomic number (Z) but different mass numbers (A). Since Z is the same, they have the same number of protons — the difference in mass comes from a different number of neutrons.

Hydrogen isotopeSymbolProtonsNeutronsElectronsAbundance in nature
Protium¹₁H101≈ 99.98%
Deuterium²₁H111≈ 0.015%
Tritium³₁H121traces (very small amounts)

Carbon also has three isotopes: ¹²₆C, ¹³₆C and ¹⁴₆C — all with 6 protons but 6, 7 and 8 neutrons respectively.

🧪
Why isotopes behave almost the same in chemistry: all three isotopes of hydrogen have 1 electron in the K-shell, so they have the same electronic configuration. Chemical properties depend mainly on valence electrons — so chemical properties are the same. But the masses differ, so physical properties such as boiling point, melting point and density are slightly different.

Uses of isotopes

IsotopeWhere it is used
²³⁵₉₂U (uranium-235)Fuel in nuclear reactors
⁶⁰₂₇Co (cobalt-60)Radiation therapy to treat cancer
¹³¹₅₃I (iodine-131)Treatment of goitre and thyroid cancer
¹⁴₆C (carbon-14)Archaeology and geology — dating ancient fossils and artefacts
🔬
Unified atomic mass unit ‘u’: to talk about such tiny masses, scientists use one atom of carbon-12 as the standard, and one-twelfth of its mass is called 1 unified atomic mass unit (1 u).

Average atomic mass

Most elements occur in nature as a mixture of isotopes. The atomic mass of such an element is the weighted average of the masses of its isotopes, taking their abundance into account.

❌ Simple mean (wrong for chlorine)

(35 + 37) ÷ 2 = 36 u. This is wrong because it treats both isotopes as equally common — but they are not.

✅ Weighted average (correct)

Chlorine is 75% ³⁵₁₇Cl and 25% ³⁷₁₇Cl.
(75/100 × 35) + (25/100 × 37) = 26.25 + 9.25 = 35.5 u.

no single chlorine atom weighs 35.5 u — it is only the average(abundance%% × mass of isotope 1) + (abundance%% × mass of isotope 2) + … ÷ 100

Isobars

Isobars are atoms of different elements that have the same mass number (A) but different atomic numbers (Z).

ElementZ (protons)NeutronsA = Z + n
Argon (Ar)182240
Potassium (K)192140
Calcium (Ca)202040
📊
Isotopes vs isobars in one line: isotopes — same Z, different A. Isobars — same A, different Z.

And what comes next?

Bohr's shells are extremely useful, but they are not the final word. The modern quantum mechanical model says that we cannot say exactly where an electron is; we can only talk about the probability of finding it around the nucleus. This gives a fuzzy picture called the electron cloud. Powerful instruments like the Scanning Tunnelling Microscope (STM) and Transmission Electron Microscope (TEM) can now actually “see” atoms.

💬Ask & Understand

Q1What are isotopes? Give one example.Very short answer▾

Isotopes are atoms of the same element that have the same atomic number but different mass numbers. For example hydrogen has three isotopes: protium (¹₁H), deuterium (²₁H) and tritium (³₁H).

Q2What are isobars? Give an example.Very short answer▾

Isobars are atoms of different elements that have the same mass number but different atomic numbers. For example calcium (Z = 20), potassium (Z = 19) and argon (Z = 18) all have a mass number of 40.

Q3Why do isotopes of the same element have similar chemical properties but different physical properties? Give one application of any isotope.Short answer▾

Isotopes have the same number of electrons and the same electronic configuration, and chemical properties depend mainly on valence electrons — so their chemical properties are similar. Their masses differ because of different numbers of neutrons, so physical properties such as boiling point and melting point differ.

Application: carbon-14 is used in carbon dating to find the age of ancient fossils and artefacts in archaeology and geology.

Q4What is average atomic mass? Chlorine exists as ³⁵₁₇Cl (75%) and ³⁷₁₇Cl (25%). Calculate its weighted average atomic mass.Numerical▾

The average atomic mass of a natural element is the weighted average of the masses of all its naturally occurring isotopes, taking their relative (percent) abundances into account. It is more accurate than a simple arithmetic mean because isotopes do not occur in equal proportions.

Calculation: (75/100 × 35) + (25/100 × 37) = 26.25 + 9.25 = 35.5 u.

Q5Pause & Ponder: Two different atoms have 11 protons each, but one has 12 neutrons and the other has 13 neutrons. How do their atomic numbers and mass numbers compare? Are they the same element?Pause & Ponder▾

Both have 11 protons, so both have atomic number 11 — they are both sodium (Na), so they are the same element.

Mass number of the first = 11 + 12 = 23; of the second = 11 + 13 = 24.

So their atomic numbers are the same but their mass numbers are different — they are isotopes of sodium.

📘Book Questions — Explained

1Define isotopes and isobars. Write the electronic configuration of the three isotopes of hydrogen. State two similarities and two differences between isotopes and isobars.

Isotopes: atoms of the same element having the same atomic number (Z) but different mass numbers (A) because of different numbers of neutrons.

Isobars: atoms of different elements having the same mass number (A) but different atomic numbers (Z).

IsotopeSymbolProtonsNeutronsElectronsConfiguration
Protium¹₁H101K: 1
Deuterium²₁H111K: 1
Tritium³₁H121K: 1

All three have the same electronic configuration, so the same chemical properties.

Two similarities: both are groupings of atoms that differ in nuclear composition; both are used to explain why atoms of the same or different elements can have varying masses.

Two differences: isotopes have the same Z but different A; isobars have different Z but the same A.

Long answer
2R3 Q3. The composition of the nuclei of three atomic species is: X — 18 protons, 19 neutrons; Y — 17 protons, 18 neutrons; Z — 17 protons, 20 neutrons. Explain the relation between (i) Y and Z and (ii) Z and X.

First find the mass numbers: X = 18 + 19 = 37; Y = 17 + 18 = 35; Z = 17 + 20 = 37.

(i) Y and Z: both have 17 protons (Z = 17), so both are chlorine (Cl). But their mass numbers are different (35 and 37), so Y and Z are isotopes of each other.

(ii) Z and X: Z has Z = 17 (chlorine) while X has Z = 18 (argon) — different elements. But both have mass number 37, so Z and X are isobars.

Revise, Reflect, Refine · Q3
3R3 Q18. If a bromine atom is available as two isotopes, ⁷⁹₃₅Br (49.7%) and ⁸¹₃₅Br (50.3%), calculate the average atomic mass of the bromine atom.

Weighted average atomic mass = (mass of first isotope × its %%/100) + (mass of second isotope × its %%/100)

= (79 × 49.7/100) + (81 × 50.3/100) = (79 × 0.497) + (81 × 0.503)

= 39.263 + 40.743 = 80.006 u ≈ 80 u.

Answer: the average atomic mass of bromine is approximately 80 u.

4Case-based: (a) Define isotopes with one example in notation. (b) Why do isotopes have similar chemical but different physical properties? Give one application. (c) Distinguish isotopes and isobars with one example each.

(a) Isotopes are atoms of the same element with the same atomic number but different mass numbers. Example: ¹²₆C and ¹⁴₆C — both have 6 protons but differ in neutrons.

(b) They have the same number of electrons and the same electronic configuration, and chemical properties depend mainly on valence electrons. Physical properties differ because the masses differ. Application: ¹⁴₆C is used in carbon dating of ancient fossils and artefacts.

(c) Isotopes: same Z, different A (example ³⁵₁₇Cl and ³⁷₁₇Cl). Isobars: different Z, same A (example ⁴⁰₂₀Ca and ⁴⁰₁₈Ar).
