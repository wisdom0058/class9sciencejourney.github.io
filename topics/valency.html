Combining Capacity of an Atom: Valency

Valence electrons, the octet idea, and how valency is worked out.

📘Lesson — The Combining Capacity of an Atom

Valence electrons

The electrons present in the outermost shell of an atom are called valence electrons, and that outermost shell is called the valence shell. Only these electrons take part in chemical reactions and in bonding, so they decide the chemical properties of an element.

Why atoms combine at all

Atoms want a stable, complete outermost shell. That usually means 8 electrons in the outermost shell — the octet. (Helium, with 2 electrons in its only shell, is complete and stable too.) Atoms that cannot reach an octet on their own combine with other atoms, giving rise to compounds.

Valency

= the number of electrons lost, gained or shared to complete its octetValency = the combining capacity of an atom

Fewer than 4 valence electrons → LOSE

Metals like sodium lose electrons. Na has 1 valence electron and loses 1 → valency 1. Magnesium has 2 → valency 2.

More than 4 valence electrons → GAIN

Non-metals gain. Oxygen has 6 valence electrons and gains 2 → valency 2. Chlorine has 7 and gains 1 → valency 1.

Exactly 4 valence electrons → SHARE

Carbon has 4 valence electrons, so it shares 4 → valency 4.

Full outermost shell → valency 0

Neon (2, 8) and argon (2, 8, 8) already have an octet, so they do not combine — valency 0.

See it in real compounds

  • H₂O (water): one oxygen atom holds two hydrogen atoms, so the valency of oxygen is 2 and of hydrogen is 1.
  • NH₃ (ammonia): one nitrogen atom holds three hydrogen atoms → valency of nitrogen is 3.
  • MgCl₂ (magnesium chloride): one magnesium atom holds two chlorine atoms → valency of magnesium is 2.
  • CH₄ (methane): one carbon atom holds four hydrogen atoms → valency of carbon is 4.
💡Sodium has electronic configuration 2, 8, 1. What is its valency?
  • The outermost shell has only 1 electron, which is fewer than 4.
  • So sodium tends to lose that 1 electron to reach the stable 2, 8 configuration.
  • Number of electrons lost = valency.

Answer: Valency of sodium = 1

💡Oxygen has electronic configuration 2, 6. What is its valency?
  • The outermost shell has 6 electrons, which is more than 4.
  • So oxygen gains electrons to complete its octet: 8 − 6 = 2 electrons.
  • Number of electrons gained = valency.

Answer: Valency of oxygen = 2

💬Ask & Understand

Q1What are valence electrons, and what is the valence shell?Very short answer▾

The electrons present in the outermost shell of an atom are called valence electrons. The outermost shell itself is known as the valence shell. Chemical properties of elements depend on the number of valence electrons.

Q2Define the valency of an element.Very short answer▾

Valency is the combining capacity of an atom. It is equal to the number of electrons gained, lost or shared by an atom to complete its octet and become stable.

Q3What is valency? Explain with the examples of sodium and oxygen how valency is determined from the electronic configuration.Short answer▾

Valency is the number of electrons an atom gains, loses or shares to complete its outermost shell (octet) and become stable — its combining capacity.

Sodium (2, 8, 1): the outermost shell has only 1 electron. Since fewer than 4 valence electrons are present, sodium tends to lose 1 electron → valency 1.

Oxygen (2, 6): the outermost shell has 6 electrons. Since more than 4 valence electrons are present, oxygen tends to gain 2 electrons → valency 2.

Q4Long answer Q5: An atom of element X has a mass number of 31 and 16 neutrons. Find its atomic number, identify the element, write its electronic configuration, find its valence electrons and valency, and write the standard notation.Long answer▾

(i) A = 31, neutrons = 16 → Z = 31 − 16 = 15.

(ii) The element with atomic number 15 is phosphorus (P).

(iii) 15 electrons → K = 2, L = 8, M = 5 → configuration 2, 8, 5.

(iv) The outermost (M) shell has 5 valence electrons. Since this is more than 4, phosphorus gains 3 electrons to complete its octet, so its valency is 3.

(v) Standard notation: ³¹₁₅P.

📘Book Questions — Explained

1R3 Q14. An element X has mass number 35 and contains 18 neutrons. (i) How many electrons and protons does X have? (ii) What is its atomic number? (iii) Identify X. (iv) Write its electronic configuration. (v) How many valence electrons does it have? (vi) What will be its mass number if two neutrons are added? (vii) What is the relation of X with the new atom?

(i) Protons = A − neutrons = 35 − 18 = 17; electrons = protons = 17.

(ii) Atomic number = number of protons = 17.

(iii) The element with Z = 17 is chlorine (Cl).

(iv) 17 electrons → K = 2, L = 8, M = 7 → configuration 2, 8, 7.

(v) The outermost (M) shell has 7 valence electrons.

(vi) New neutrons = 18 + 2 = 20, so new A = 17 + 20 = 37.

(vii) Both atoms have Z = 17 but A = 35 and 37, so X and the new atom are isotopes of each other — the same element with different mass numbers.

Revise, Reflect, Refine · Q14
2R3 Q15. An atom has 12 protons and 12 neutrons in the nucleus. Imagine all the electrons are replaced by hypothetical particles with the same charge as an electron but 500 times heavier. What effect will this have on (i) the atomic number, (ii) the atomic mass, (iii) the mass number and (iv) the overall charge?

(i) No change. The atomic number depends only on the number of protons, which is still 12.

(ii) Increases. Normally electrons have negligible mass; particles 500 times heavier add significant extra mass to the atom.

(iii) No change. Mass number counts only protons and neutrons (12 + 12 = 24). Electrons are not part of the mass number.

(iv) No change. The new particles carry the same −1 charge each, and there are still 12 of them, so the total negative charge is −12. With +12 from the protons the atom stays electrically neutral.

Revise, Reflect, Refine · Q15
3What happens when an atom loses or gains electrons? Does its atomic number change?

When an atom loses electrons it becomes a positive ion (cation) and when it gains electrons it becomes a negative ion (anion). Its atomic number does not change, because the number of protons stays the same — only the number of electrons changes. For example, Na (2, 8, 1) loses one electron to become Na⁺ with 11 protons but 10 electrons.
