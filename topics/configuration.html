How Are Electrons Distributed in Shells?

Bohr–Bury rules, 2n², the octet rule and stepwise filling.

2n²OctetK L M NConfigurations

📘Lesson — Filling the Shells: The Bohr–Bury Rules

Where exactly do the electrons sit? In 1921 Bohr and Bury gave the same set of rules for distributing electrons in the shells (energy levels).

1️⃣ Formula 2n²

The maximum number of electrons a shell can hold is 2n², where n is the shell number. K (n = 1) → 2, L (n = 2) → 8, M (n = 3) → 18.

2️⃣ Outer limit is 8

The outermost shell can never hold more than 8 electrons — even if 2n² allows more. (If the first shell itself is the outermost shell, it can hold a maximum of only 2.)

3️⃣ Fill from inside out

Shells are filled step by step: K, then L, then M, then N. The next shell starts only after the inner shell is complete.

4️⃣ Exceptions exist

There are a few exceptions to rules 2 and 3 for heavier atoms, which you will study in higher classes.

K (n=1) = 2  ·  L (n=2) = 8  ·  M (n=3) = 18  ·  N (n=4) = 322n²

Electronic configuration

The distribution of electrons in the various shells of an atom is called its electronic configuration. We write it as a simple list of numbers, e.g. sodium is written as 2, 8, 1.

💡Write the electronic configuration of nitrogen, oxygen, fluorine and neon (atomic numbers 7, 8, 9, 10).
  • The K-shell holds 2 electrons first. Electrons left over: N → 5, O → 6, F → 7, Ne → 8.
  • All these leftover electrons go into the L-shell.
  • N = 2, 5  ·  O = 2, 6  ·  F = 2, 7  ·  Ne = 2, 8.

Answer: N: 2,5  ·  O: 2,6  ·  F: 2,7  ·  Ne: 2,8

💡Write the electronic configuration of sodium (Z = 11) and magnesium (Z = 12).
  • K holds 2 and L holds 8, so K + L = 10 electrons.
  • Sodium has 11 − 10 = 1 electron left; magnesium has 12 − 10 = 2 electrons left.
  • The remaining electrons go into the M-shell.

Answer: Na: K=2, L=8, M=1 → 2, 8, 1  ·  Mg: K=2, L=8, M=2 → 2, 8, 2

💬Ask & Understand

Q1What is the maximum number of electrons that can be filled in the K, L and M shells?Very short answer▾

Using the formula 2n²: K-shell (n = 1) holds 2 electrons, L-shell (n = 2) holds 8 electrons, and M-shell (n = 3) holds 18 electrons.

Q2What are the rules given by Bohr and Bury for distributing electrons in the shells of an atom?Short answer▾

(1) The maximum number of electrons in any shell is given by 2n²: K = 2, L = 8, M = 18. (2) The maximum number of electrons in the outermost shell is 8 (the first shell can hold a maximum of 2). (3) Electrons are filled in a stepwise manner, starting from the shell closest to the nucleus (K, then L, M, N…). The next shell begins filling only after the inner shell is complete.

Q3Pause & Ponder: Write the electronic configuration of the elements with atomic numbers 12, 16 and 18.Numerical · Pause & Ponder▾

• Z = 12 (Magnesium): 2, 8, 2 — K = 2, L = 8, M = 2.
• Z = 16 (Sulfur): 2, 8, 6 — K = 2, L = 8, M = 6.
• Z = 18 (Argon): 2, 8, 8 — K = 2, L = 8, M = 8.

Q4Pause & Ponder: Identify the number of electrons in the outermost shell of ¹²₆C, ¹⁹₉F and ²⁸₁₄Si.Numerical · Pause & Ponder▾

Carbon (Z = 6): configuration 2, 4 → the outermost shell is L and it has 4 electrons.

Fluorine (Z = 9): configuration 2, 7 → the outermost shell is L and it has 7 electrons.

Silicon (Z = 14): configuration 2, 8, 4 → the outermost shell is M and it has 4 electrons.

📘Book Questions — Explained

1R3 Q8. Magnesium is essential for many biological processes, including muscle contraction. For an atom of magnesium with mass number 24 and atomic number 12, determine the number of (i) protons, (ii) neutrons, (iii) electrons, and the arrangement of electrons.

Mass number A = 24, atomic number Z = 12.

(i) Protons = Z = 12.
(ii) Neutrons = A − Z = 24 − 12 = 12.
(iii) Electrons = protons (neutral atom) = 12.

Arrangement of electrons: K-shell = 2 (filled first, maximum 2), L-shell = 8 (maximum 8), M-shell = 2 (the remaining electrons).

Electronic configuration: 2, 8, 2.
