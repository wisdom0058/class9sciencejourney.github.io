Rediscovering the Roots of Atomic Theory

How ancient thinkers in India and Greece imagined the tiniest bits of matter.

KanadaparmanuatomosDalton 1808
📘 Lesson🧪 Demo Lab🎯 Quick Quiz💬 Ask & Understand📘 Book Questions

📘Lesson — The First Ideas About the Smallest Piece of Matter

Take a piece of chalk and break it. Break the pieces again. Keep breaking. For a long time people believed that you must finally reach a piece so small that it cannot be broken any further. That final piece was given a name: the atom.

India: Acharya Kanada and the parmanu

Acharya Kanada, an ancient Indian thinker, asked a very modern question: if I keep dividing matter (dravya), will the division ever stop? He reasoned that it must stop somewhere. The smallest indivisible particle that remains he called a parmanu. His ideas are recorded in the Sanskrit text Vaisesika Sutras.

  • A parmanu is infinitely small and cannot be perceived by our senses.
  • Parmanu do not stay alone. They join in pairs (dyads) and triplets (triads).
  • These combinations build up every material substance we see around us.

Greece: Leucippus and Democritus

Far away, two Greek philosophers thought along surprisingly similar lines. They said matter is made of tiny indivisible particles and named them atomos — a Greek word meaning “indivisible” or “cannot be cut”.

💡
Both ideas were imagination, not experiment. Kanada and the Greek thinkers had no laboratory proof. They reasoned with logic and philosophy. That is why they are called philosophical ideas, not scientific theories.

1808: John Dalton makes it scientific

Centuries later, John Dalton studied the experiments of his time and put forward the first scientific atomic theory. His main points were:

🧱 All matter is made of atoms

Every substance is built from extremely small particles called atoms.

✂️ Atoms are indivisible

An atom cannot be broken into smaller parts — this was Dalton's strong belief.

🏗️ Atoms are the building blocks

They are the fundamental units out of which matter is made.

🐾 Atoms of one element are alike

Atoms of the same element are identical; atoms of different elements are different.

Dalton's theory was special because it was based on experiments, not just imagination. It also created new questions: What are atoms made of? What do they look like? What makes the atoms of one element different from another? Chasing these questions led to the discovery of smaller particles inside the atom.

Late 1800s: radioactivity breaks the “indivisible” atom

Scientists found that some elements give out invisible energy and particles all by themselves. This is called radioactivity. If energy and particles are coming out of an atom, then the atom cannot be one solid indivisible ball. Dalton's theory was in trouble — and a great adventure in science began.

💬Ask & Understand

Q1Who first used the term atomos, and what does it mean?Very short answer▾

The Greek philosophers Leucippus and Democritus used the term atomos. In Greek it means indivisible — something that cannot be cut or divided further.

Q2What did Acharya Kanada call the smallest indivisible particles, and where are his ideas recorded?Very short answer▾

He called them parmanus. His ideas are recorded in the Sanskrit text Vaisesika Sutras. According to him a parmanu is infinitely small and cannot be perceived by the senses, and parmanus combine in pairs (dyads) and triplets (triads) to form all material substances.

Q3In which year did Dalton propose his atomic theory, and what was its main idea?Very short answer▾

In 1808. Dalton proposed that all matter is composed of indivisible particles called atoms, which are the fundamental building blocks of matter. Atoms of the same element are identical, and atoms of different elements are different.

Q4What is radioactivity, and how did its discovery challenge Dalton's atomic theory?Short answer▾

In the late 19th century scientists found that certain elements emit invisible energy and particles spontaneously. This phenomenon is called radioactivity.

Until then atoms were believed to be the smallest indivisible units of matter, as Dalton had proposed. But if energy and particles are being emitted from within the atom itself, the atom must be made of even smaller subatomic particles. This directly challenged Dalton's theory and laid the foundation for Thomson's and Rutherford's work.

📘Book Questions — Explained

1Think it over: Are atoms the smallest indivisible particles?

No. Atoms are not the smallest indivisible particles. They are made up of smaller subatomic particles — electrons, protons and neutrons. The idea of an indivisible atom was only a belief until experiments on radioactivity showed that particles can come out of an atom.

NCERT · Think It Over (page 140)
2Think it over: Why did scientists keep modifying atomic models?

Because new experiments and discoveries kept revealing limitations in earlier models. Each model explained some observations but failed at others. To explain the new results better, scientists proposed improved models — Dalton → Thomson → Rutherford → Bohr → the modern electron-cloud model.

NCERT · Think It Over (page 140)
3Case-based: Priya reads that Kanada spoke of parmanu, the Greeks spoke of atomos, and Dalton (1808) described atoms as indivisible building blocks, until radioactivity showed atoms are made of smaller particles. (a) What did Dalton propose about atoms? (b) What did radioactivity prove and how did it challenge Dalton? (c) What name did the Greek philosophers give to the indivisible particles, and what does it mean?

(a) Dalton proposed that all matter is made of indivisible particles called atoms, the fundamental building blocks of matter that cannot be broken into smaller parts.

(b) Radioactivity proved that atoms are made of smaller subatomic particles. Since particles and energy come out of the atom itself, the atom cannot be indivisible — which directly challenged Dalton's theory.

(c) They called them atomos. In Greek, atomos means indivisible.
